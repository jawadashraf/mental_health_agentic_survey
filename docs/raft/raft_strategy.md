About The Raft


### Vision, mission, values

Our**vision** is for care-experienced children and families in Leicestershire to feel understood, valued and empowered to reach their full potential.

Our**mission** is to journey alongside Leicestershire’s care experienced children and families through providing trauma informed activities in three key areas: Children and Youth; Adults; and Training and Development with the aim of promoting recovery from early childhood trauma.



| Value | What does this look like in The Raft |
| --- | --- |
| We are living it | We hear and understand the needs of care experienced children and those that support them. As a team who have lived experience in fostering and adoption, we get what it is like to care for children who have experienced trauma. We are willing to learn, adapt and shape our activities according to these needs and the feedback we receive from our beneficiaries. |
| We’re together | The Raft is a supportive community, aiming to navigate life alongside each other. We don’t always have the answers; our purpose is to share ideas, experiences, to hear each other’s stories and to be encouraging. |
| We’re safe | We promote belonging, understanding and compassion. It is important that safety and felt safety is foundational to all that we do. This includes ensuring we have rigorous safeguarding processes in place, and embedding trauma-informed awareness and practice across all our work. |
| We’re informed | We know and we work with relevant agencies, organisations and projects across Leicestershire to enable appropriate and safe signposting / referral. We are aware of the landscape around care, fostering and adoption, and data, polices and reports that could influence our work. We will undertake regular training and continual professional development. |
| We’re fun | We are committed to creating and celebrating moments of enjoyment and laughter. |



### History

The history of The Raft starts in 2004 when Ruth Crook (founding trustee of The Raft) had a vision of a country house or place where families could receive care, training, support and preparation for life. A decade later, in 2014, Debbie Hill (foster carer and Home for Good Leicester) had a vision of providing a respite place for foster families.

In 2016, Sarah Gamble (who was working for Safe Families in Leicester) and Debbie Hill started to have conversations about caring for the vulnerable and the provision of trauma care by churches. This led to an initial meeting, on 22nd February 2018, with Michelle Nelson (Adoption UK / Adopter Voice Champion), Debbie Hill, Sarah Gamble, Esther Clark (All Nations Children’s Worker), Sharon Lesson (Adopter, employed Family Worker at St Johns church) and Ruth Crook (TLG, Teacher, Trainee Counsellor and Kintsugi). We all had a similar heart – to work with young people and families impacted by trauma and to encourage churches to respond.  The leaflet we produced at this time speaks to this – we introduced the three charities we were initially supporting, praying for, advocating for and bringing together – TLG, Safe Families and Home for Good. At this time the verse 1 John 4:19 became very special *“We love because he first loved us”.*

In the early days we organised prayer meetings for all involved in these types of ministries. We welcomed Lisa Squires (Safe Families) in June 2019 and Julia Hamer (adopter) in Autumn 2019. We organised our first Raft conference in September 2019 – the speaker was Kat Osborn, CEO of Safe Families. We held prayer breakfasts, a trauma training evening with Home for Good at Hope Community Church and we met with our own church leaders.

During 2020 we decided to register The Raft as a charity, becoming registered on the 18th June 2021. Sharon, Ruth, Julia and Debbie were the founding trustees, with Paul Cromwell and Chris Plummer becoming trustees in summer 2023 and Carys joining the trustee team in January 2025. We now have a growing team of volunteers, still committed to that original vision of 20 years ago – a place in Leicestershire, The Raft Centre, for care-experienced children and their families.


### Where we are now

As of Summer 2026, we have a volunteer team of 7 people which includes our four trustees. One of our trustees, Chris, is also our treasurer, and another trustee, Paul, is our safeguarding lead.

Our charity is experiencing steady growth and now delivers activities across three core strands: children, adults, and training and development. We are committed to offering specialist support for careexperienced children, addressing the impacts of trauma. We also provide targeted wellbeing and community support for caregivers, and we meet the ongoing training and development needs of all those engaged in this work.

## Our offer

### Children and Youth

Our current offer to children includes our Brain Builders project. CookStart and parties / celebrations.

- *Brain Builders**
Our Brain Builders project uses Lego Serious Play (LSP), a facilitated methodology that encourages participants to express ideas and concepts using Lego bricks rather than relying solely on verbal communication. This can lead to more effective communication. When we build, we engage the part of the brain that generates sensory images in our minds. This helps us to work out our ideas and develop deeper understanding. It helps with problem solving, forming narrative and reflection.

In 2025, two Raft volunteers were trained on LSP and they are now working on delivering Brain Builders sessions to care-experienced children, with the aim of running these sessions in a variety of settings (schools, after school clubs, churches, residential children’s homes etc).  A further aim is to develop a set of resources and a toolkit explaining our approach, which can then be used to train other practitioners.

- *Cook Start**
Cook Start supports **careexperienced young people aged 14 to 18** to develop the practical skills and confidence needed to prepare **costeffective, nutritious, and enjoyable meals.** Cooking independently is a fundamental life skill that contributes to longterm wellbeing, resilience, and selfsufficiency.

Parents and carers are encouraged to attend sessions alongside the young person, helping to **strengthen attachment bonds** and reinforce learning at home. Our aim is to expand Cook Start by offering additional sessions that build participants’ confidence, broaden their recipe portfolio, and equip them with skills they can carry forward into adulthood.

- *Parties**
We offer a range of events throughout the year, including our annual Christmas Party and Summer Pool Party. These events provide valuable opportunities for care-experienced young people to spend quality time together, build friendships, strengthen connections, and have fun in a supportive environment. Our aim is to continue offering these much-loved events, helping to reduce isolation and promote a sense of community and belonging.


### Adults

Our current provision for adults includes Kintsugi courses, wellbeing events and a regular podcast.

- *Kintsugi**
Kintsugi Hope is a mental health and wellbeing charity that provides courses for adults and youth on various topics such as depression, anxiety, anger, shame, healthy relations and resilience. We offer these courses to foster carers and adopters, and we hope to offer them to teenage children over time.

- *Well-being events**
We run regular craft and wellbeing sessions for parents and carers. These tend to include a craft activity, hand massages and time to chat over refreshments.

- *Podcasts**
Faith That Cares is a podcast, currently available on Spotify, that brings together conversations on fostering, adoption and the Christian faith. It started in summer of 2024 and releases episodes approximately every month. Episodes are recorded on Zoom and edited with Descript (the basis platform is available for free).

Key strategic aims for Faith That Cares:

- Promote podcast to a wider audience
- Add Faith That Cares to more hosting platforms
- Look at ways of involving the care experienced community
- Expand the Faith That Cares concept into resources, events and training.

### Training and Development

We currently offer our beneficiaries a range of courses and resources, including trauma training and interactive videos.

- *Courses**
We deliver a range of training courses for adults looking after care-experienced children and organisations, such as churches, ideally at low or no cost, with a self-care element included so that we can reduce the risk of secondary trauma / re-traumatisation. Topics could include: child on parent violence, youth mental health, NVR, neuro-diversity, compassion fatigue etc. The aim is to equip parents, carers and others in their care-giving role to help promote family stability and increase skills and confidence.

Strategic aims:

- Continue to research training needs, perhaps using AI surveying tools
- Offer a variety of training in different formats and time slots
- Potentially establish a “learning community or hub” where expertise is shared.
- *Trauma training for churches**
We offer trauma training sessions for churches to raise awareness of trauma informed practices and the needs of the care-experienced community.

- Our strategic aim for this year is to deliver more courses to churches and to identify a possible funding stream from these courses.
- A further aim would be to engage on a church engagement “tour” and use this as an opportunity to promote our training offer.
- *Resources**
We have developed a suite of six introductory training videos covering key topics including safeguarding, traumainformed language, neuroscience, stress responses, and attachment. These resources are available to individuals and organisations seeking foundational knowledge or an accessible entry point into these areas of practice.

Our strategic intention is to make these training materials freely available while inviting voluntary donations to the Raft to support the continued development and delivery of our work.


### Exploration of emerging ideas, research and opportunities

We recognise that the science around trauma, attachment and neurodiversity is constantly evolving, the local care-experienced community is often in flux with families changing as children move and develop, and that we as a charity need to be responding to updated research, emerging themes and needs.

As a small charity, we want to be responsive to new ideas and able to respond quickly where it is appropriate. We are open to hearing creative ways of supporting our community to heal from past trauma and we are open to new partnership opportunities, recognising the unique skills, influence and resources of those we work alongside.

We want to develop a culture within the Raft for our trustees and volunteers to feel able to question existing practice and suggest ways of improvement. We want to invest in our trustees and volunteers, increasing their own knowledge and skills, as well as being able to access appropriate care as we respond to the significant needs of our beneficiaries.

## Our brand and messaging

In 2024 we worked with Graffio to develop a logo and branding that reflected our ethos of being family friendly and accessible, linked to the idea of a “raft” being a collective noun for a group of penguins.

The brand kit they developed for us has a colour palette and font, and is used on our website, social media and on all our materials and printing.

We are committed to using affirming, positive, and inclusive language that recognises the strengths, identities, and lived experiences of careexperienced children and their families. Our approach emphasises collaboration, empathy, and psychological safety, ensuring that every interaction promotes dignity, agency, and belonging. We prioritise strengthbased language, traumainformed communication, and terminology that reflects diverse family structures and trusted relationships. This intentional use of language supports our mission to create safe, supportive environments where children and families feel valued, respected, and empowered.
